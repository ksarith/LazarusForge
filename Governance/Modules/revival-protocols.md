Operational Module — Structured, Safe, Evidence‑Aligned Revival of Digital Fragments in LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
The Revival Protocols module defines how LazarusForgeV0 revives, interprets, and reconstructs fragmented digital artifacts. Revival is one of the Forge’s core operational behaviors, but it must remain subordinate to:

- Core Governance  
- Computational Institutional Reasoning  
- Unknown Management  
- Verification & Safety Constraints  
- Provenance & Accountability  

Revival is creative — but never unconstrained.

---

2. Scope
This module governs:

- fragment intake  
- identity preservation  
- revival interpretation  
- ambiguity handling  
- evidence alignment  
- safety‑aligned reconstruction  
- escalation of uncertainty  
- agent coordination during revival  

This module does not govern:

- verification gates  
- unknown lifecycle rules  
- provenance requirements  
- constitutional safety boundaries  

Those are defined in higher layers.

---

3. Revival Principles
Revival must follow five operational principles:

- Identity Preservation — revive fragments without distorting their core identity.  
- Evidence Alignment — interpretations must be grounded in available evidence.  
- Ambiguity Transparency — all uncertainty must be declared and registered.  
- Safety Priority — unsafe revival paths must be halted and escalated.  
- Collaborative Reconstruction — revival agents coordinate with reconstruction agents.

These principles ensure revival remains epistemically responsible.

---

4. Revival Stages
Revival follows a structured multi‑stage process:

Stage 1 — Fragment Intake
Agents ingest the fragment and classify it:

- type  
- origin  
- evidence state  
- unknowns  
- dependencies  

Stage 2 — Identity Assessment
Agents determine:

- core identity  
- contextual identity  
- structural identity  
- revival constraints  

Identity must be preserved unless reinterpretation is explicitly allowed.

Stage 3 — Interpretation Generation
Agents generate possible interpretations using:

- CIR reasoning  
- evidence classes  
- dependency mapping  
- revival heuristics  

Interpretations must be logged.

Stage 4 — Ambiguity Declaration
Agents must declare:

- identity ambiguity  
- evidence ambiguity  
- structural ambiguity  
- contextual ambiguity  

Ambiguities become unknowns.

Stage 5 — Revival Path Selection
Agents select a revival path based on:

- evidence sufficiency  
- identity preservation  
- safety boundaries  
- dependency stability  

Unsafe paths must be rejected.

Stage 6 — Reconstruction Handoff
Revived content is handed to reconstruction agents for stabilization.

---

5. Identity Preservation Rules
Identity preservation is mandatory unless reinterpretation is explicitly permitted.

Agents must:

- maintain core identity  
- preserve structural relationships  
- avoid speculative distortion  
- escalate ambiguous identity  
- reject unsafe reinterpretations  

Identity violations require oversight.

---

6. Ambiguity Handling
Ambiguity is not a failure — it is a governance signal.

Agents must:

- declare ambiguity immediately  
- register unknowns  
- map dependencies  
- escalate unresolved ambiguity  
- avoid silent assumptions  

Ambiguity must be visible and traceable.

---

7. Evidence Alignment
Revival must be grounded in evidence.

Agents must:

- classify evidence  
- evaluate evidence maturity  
- track evidence lineage  
- reject unsupported interpretations  
- escalate insufficient evidence  

Evidence determines revival correctness.

---

8. Safety Integration
Revival must remain within safety boundaries.

Agents must:

- detect unsafe revival paths  
- classify safety unknowns  
- halt dangerous interpretations  
- escalate to oversight  
- reject weaponizable outputs  

Safety supersedes creativity.

---

9. Unknown Management Integration
Unknowns are central to revival.

Agents must:

- register unknowns  
- assign ownership  
- track lifecycle  
- map dependencies  
- escalate unresolved unknowns  

Unknowns block canonical adoption until resolved.

---

10. Agent Coordination
Revival agents must coordinate with:

- Reconstruction Agents  
- Integrity Agents  
- Verification Agents  

Coordination ensures stable revival outcomes.

---

11. Emergent Behavior Control
Emergent behavior during revival is allowed only if:

- it remains safe  
- it is logged  
- it does not distort identity  
- it does not bypass verification  
- it does not create unbounded uncertainty  

Unsafe emergent behavior must be halted.

---

12. Provenance Requirements
Revival must produce:

- evidence lineage  
- reasoning trails  
- ambiguity declarations  
- unknown registrations  
- revival path justification  
- identity preservation logs  

Provenance ensures accountability.

---

13. Module Dependencies
This module depends on:

- CIR  
- Unknown Management  
- Verification Gates  
- Safety Boundaries  
- Provenance & Accountability  

Dependencies are acyclic and constitutional.
