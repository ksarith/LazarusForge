reconstruction-rules.md

Operational Module — Stabilization, Structural Maturation, and Canonical Integration of Revived Content in LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
The Reconstruction Rules module defines how revived fragments are stabilized, matured, structured, and prepared for canonical integration. Reconstruction is the bridge between:

- revival (creative interpretation),  
- verification (epistemic validation),  
- maturity classification (developmental progression),  
- provenance (historical traceability),  
- unknown management (uncertainty resolution).

Reconstruction is operational — but governed by:

- Core Governance  
- Computational Institutional Reasoning  
- Unknown Management  
- Verification & Safety Constraints  
- Provenance & Accountability  

---

2. Scope
This module governs:

- stabilization of revived content  
- structural reconstruction  
- dependency restoration  
- ambiguity resolution  
- maturity advancement  
- preparation for verification gates  
- coordination with integrity agents  

This module does not govern:

- revival interpretation  
- verification gate logic  
- constitutional safety boundaries  
- provenance requirements  

Those are defined in higher layers.

---

3. Reconstruction Principles
Reconstruction must follow five operational principles:

- Structural Fidelity — preserve the fragment’s structural identity.  
- Epistemic Stability — reduce uncertainty and resolve unknowns.  
- Dependency Restoration — rebuild missing or broken relationships.  
- Safety Alignment — avoid unsafe structural transformations.  
- Provenance Preservation — record every reconstruction decision.

These principles ensure reconstruction is coherent and trustworthy.

---

4. Reconstruction Stages
Reconstruction follows a structured multi‑stage process:

Stage 1 — Stabilization
Agents stabilize revived content by:

- resolving immediate inconsistencies  
- identifying structural unknowns  
- mapping dependencies  
- preserving identity constraints

Stage 2 — Structural Reconstruction
Agents rebuild:

- missing components  
- broken relationships  
- incomplete structures  
- contextual anchors

Reconstruction must remain evidence‑aligned.

Stage 3 — Ambiguity Resolution
Agents resolve:

- structural ambiguity  
- contextual ambiguity  
- relational ambiguity  
- identity ambiguity (if allowed)

Unresolved ambiguity becomes unknowns.

Stage 4 — Maturity Advancement
Agents advance the element from:

- Exploratory → Provisional  
- Provisional → Verified  

Advancement requires evidence maturation and dependency stability.

Stage 5 — Verification Preparation
Agents prepare the element for verification gates by:

- completing reasoning trails  
- validating provenance  
- resolving unknowns  
- confirming safety boundaries

Stage 6 — Canonical Integration (if verified)
Verified elements are prepared for canonical promotion.

---

5. Structural Fidelity Rules
Reconstruction must preserve:

- core identity  
- structural relationships  
- contextual meaning  
- architectural lineage

Agents may not:

- distort identity  
- introduce speculative structure  
- remove essential relationships  
- bypass ambiguity declaration

Structural fidelity is mandatory.

---

6. Dependency Restoration
Agents must restore:

- upstream dependencies  
- downstream dependencies  
- cross‑module dependencies  
- contextual anchors  
- relational structures

Dependency restoration must be evidence‑aligned and safe.

---

7. Unknown Management Integration
Unknowns are central to reconstruction.

Agents must:

- declare structural unknowns  
- assign ownership  
- map dependencies  
- track lifecycle  
- escalate unresolved unknowns  
- retire resolved unknowns with provenance

Unknowns block maturity advancement until resolved.

---

8. Ambiguity Resolution
Ambiguity must be:

- declared  
- classified  
- investigated  
- resolved or escalated  
- logged in provenance

Ambiguity cannot be ignored or silently assumed.

---

9. Safety Integration
Reconstruction must remain within safety boundaries.

Agents must:

- detect unsafe structural transformations  
- classify safety unknowns  
- halt dangerous reconstruction paths  
- escalate to oversight  
- reject weaponizable structures

Safety supersedes reconstruction freedom.

---

10. Maturity Integration
Reconstruction directly influences maturity:

- stabilization → Exploratory  
- structural reconstruction → Provisional  
- ambiguity resolution → Verified  
- verification preparation → Canonical eligibility

Maturity cannot advance without reconstruction.

---

11. Verification Preparation
Agents must prepare revived content for verification gates by:

- completing evidence lineage  
- resolving unknowns  
- validating provenance  
- ensuring dependency stability  
- confirming safety boundaries  
- completing CIR reasoning trails

Verification readiness is a reconstruction responsibility.

---

12. Agent Coordination
Reconstruction agents must coordinate with:

- Revival Agents  
- Integrity Agents  
- Verification Agents  
- Discovery Agents  

Coordination ensures stable and coherent reconstruction.

---

13. Emergent Behavior Control
Emergent behavior during reconstruction is allowed only if:

- it remains safe  
- it is logged  
- it does not distort structure  
- it does not bypass verification  
- it does not create unbounded uncertainty

Unsafe emergent behavior must be halted immediately.

---

14. Provenance Requirements
Reconstruction must produce:

- structural reasoning trails  
- dependency restoration logs  
- ambiguity resolution records  
- unknown lifecycle updates  
- maturity advancement documentation  
- verification preparation logs

Provenance ensures accountability and architectural memory.

---

15. Module Dependencies
This module depends on:

- CIR  
- Unknown Management  
- Verification Gates  
- Safety Boundaries  
- Provenance & Accountability  

Dependencies are acyclic and constitutional.
