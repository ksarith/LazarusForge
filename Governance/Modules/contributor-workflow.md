Operational Module — Contributor Responsibilities, Processes, and Epistemic Alignment in LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
The Contributor Workflow module defines how contributors participate in LazarusForgeV0 in a way that is:

- epistemically aligned,  
- safe,  
- structured,  
- provenance‑preserving,  
- uncertainty‑aware,  
- verification‑ready.

Contributors are not just editors — they are institutional participants in the Forge’s epistemic architecture.

This workflow ensures that every contribution strengthens the Forge rather than destabilizing it.

---

2. Scope
This module governs:

- contributor onboarding  
- contribution lifecycle  
- unknown declaration  
- evidence alignment  
- reasoning trail creation  
- verification preparation  
- provenance logging  
- escalation and oversight triggers  

This module does not govern:

- constitutional principles  
- verification gate definitions  
- unknown lifecycle rules  
- safety boundaries  

Those are defined in higher layers.

---

3. Contributor Principles
Contributors must follow five operational principles:

- Epistemic Responsibility — reason using CIR.  
- Uncertainty Transparency — declare unknowns immediately.  
- Evidence Alignment — support claims with structured evidence.  
- Safety Priority — avoid harmful or destabilizing changes.  
- Provenance Preservation — record all reasoning and decisions.

These principles ensure contributions remain coherent and trustworthy.

---

4. Contributor Roles
Contributors may take on one or more roles:

- Revival Contributor — revives and interprets fragments.  
- Reconstruction Contributor — stabilizes revived content.  
- Verification Contributor — prepares content for verification gates.  
- Integrity Contributor — enforces architectural invariants.  
- Discovery Contributor — locates fragments, unknowns, and dependencies.

Roles may overlap but must remain within safety boundaries.

---

5. Contribution Lifecycle
Contributions follow a structured lifecycle:

Step 1 — Intake
Contributor identifies a fragment, issue, unknown, or improvement.

Step 2 — Unknown Declaration
Contributor declares any uncertainty and registers unknowns.

Step 3 — Evidence Collection
Contributor gathers evidence and documents lineage.

Step 4 — Reasoning Trail Creation
Contributor uses CIR to generate structured reasoning.

Step 5 — Drafting
Contributor creates a draft in the sandbox or module layer.

Step 6 — Reconstruction & Stabilization
Contributor stabilizes structure and resolves ambiguity.

Step 7 — Verification Preparation
Contributor prepares the element for verification gates.

Step 8 — Oversight (if required)
Contributor escalates high‑impact changes.

Step 9 — Canonical Integration
Verified contributions may be promoted to canonical status.

---

6. Unknown Management Requirements
Contributors must integrate Unknown Management into all work:

- declare unknowns  
- assign ownership  
- map dependencies  
- track lifecycle  
- escalate unresolved unknowns  
- retire resolved unknowns with provenance

Unknowns are governance objects, not metadata.

---

7. Evidence Requirements
Contributors must:

- classify evidence  
- evaluate evidence maturity  
- track evidence lineage  
- reject unsupported claims  
- escalate insufficient evidence

Evidence determines correctness.

---

8. Reasoning Trail Requirements
Contributors must produce reasoning trails that include:

- observations  
- interpretations  
- hypotheses  
- dependency maps  
- ambiguity declarations  
- verification readiness notes

Reasoning trails prevent silent drift.

---

9. Safety Requirements
Contributors must follow Safety Boundaries:

- avoid harmful outputs  
- detect unsafe behavior  
- classify safety unknowns  
- halt dangerous changes  
- escalate risk to oversight

Safety supersedes creativity.

---

10. Provenance Requirements
Contributors must maintain:

- change history  
- evidence lineage  
- reasoning trails  
- unknown lifecycle updates  
- oversight decisions  
- architectural ancestry

Provenance ensures accountability.

---

11. Escalation Protocol
Contributors must escalate when:

- safety unknowns exist  
- emergent behavior appears  
- identity reconstruction is ambiguous  
- architectural risk increases  
- verification gates cannot be passed  
- provenance is incomplete

Escalation goes to verification contributors or oversight.

---

12. Contributor Tools (Conceptual)
Contributors rely on:

- Revival Protocols  
- Reconstruction Rules  
- Repository Integrity  
- Routing & Discovery  

These modules support contributor workflows.

---

13. Contributor Responsibilities
Contributors must:

- uphold constitutional governance  
- maintain epistemic integrity  
- preserve provenance  
- manage unknowns  
- prepare for verification  
- escalate responsibly  
- avoid destabilizing changes  
- support architectural coherence

Contributors are epistemic stewards of the Forge.
