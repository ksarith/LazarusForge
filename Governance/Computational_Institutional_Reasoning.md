Constitutional Logic for Epistemic Governance and Unknown Management in LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
Computational Institutional Reasoning (CIR) defines how the Forge thinks.

It establishes the reasoning model used by:
- governance institutions,  
- verification systems,  
- revival engines,  
- architectural decision processes,  
- unknown lifecycle management.

CIR is not operational.  
It is constitutional logic — the cognitive framework that ensures the Forge reasons consistently, safely, and transparently.

---

2. Institutional Reasoning Mission
CIR exists to ensure that LazarusForgeV0:

- reasons about uncertainty explicitly,  
- distinguishes exploration from specification,  
- evaluates evidence systematically,  
- maintains epistemic integrity,  
- governs unknowns as first‑class architectural objects,  
- supports safe and mature architectural evolution.

CIR is the ideological core of the Forge’s epistemic architecture.

---

3. Institutional Reasoning Principles
These principles define how the Forge interprets information, uncertainty, and architectural change.

3.1 Evidence‑Centric Reasoning
All institutional reasoning must:
- prioritize evidence over intuition,  
- classify evidence explicitly,  
- track provenance,  
- evaluate epistemic maturity,  
- reject unverifiable claims.

Evidence is the foundation of institutional trust.

3.2 Uncertainty as a Governance Object
Uncertainty is not noise — it is a governance signal.

CIR requires:
- explicit declaration of unknowns,  
- structured tracking,  
- lifecycle management,  
- dependency mapping,  
- retirement upon resolution.

Unknowns are constitutional entities.

3.3 Separation of Exploration and Specification
CIR enforces the boundary between:
- exploratory reasoning (creative, generative, speculative),  
- institutional reasoning (verified, mature, canonical).

Exploration is encouraged.  
Specification is controlled.

3.4 Verification as Institutional Gatekeeping
Verification is not a module — it is an institutional function.

CIR requires:
- verification gates,  
- epistemic maturity checks,  
- safety validation,  
- provenance review.

Nothing becomes canonical without verification.

3.5 Institutional Memory
Institutions must remember:
- how decisions were made,  
- what evidence was used,  
- what unknowns existed,  
- how they were resolved,  
- what architectural lineage led to the current state.

Memory prevents epistemic drift.

---

4. Institutional Reasoning Cycle
CIR defines a five‑stage reasoning cycle:

1. Observation  
   Identify fragments, evidence, unknowns, or revival candidates.

2. Interpretation  
   Classify evidence, map dependencies, evaluate uncertainty.

3. Exploration  
   Generate hypotheses, revival paths, or architectural possibilities.

4. Verification  
   Apply verification gates, safety constraints, and maturity checks.

5. Institutionalization  
   Promote verified knowledge to canonical status with provenance.

This cycle governs all architectural evolution.

---

5. Unknown Management Integration
CIR is the philosophical foundation of Unknown Management.

5.1 Unknown Registration
Unknowns must be registered when:
- evidence is incomplete,  
- dependencies are unclear,  
- revival paths are ambiguous,  
- architectural maturity is insufficient.

5.2 Unknown Ownership
Every unknown must have:
- an owner,  
- a purpose,  
- a dependency map,  
- a maturity classification.

5.3 Unknown Lifecycle
Unknowns move through:
- Declared  
- Investigated  
- Explored  
- Verified  
- Resolved  
- Retired

CIR governs transitions between these states.

5.4 Unknowns as Architectural Dependencies
Unknowns are not documentation.  
They are active governance objects that influence:
- revival decisions,  
- module behavior,  
- architectural maturity,  
- verification requirements.

---

6. Institutional Safety Logic
CIR embeds safety into reasoning itself.

6.1 Anti‑Weaponization Reasoning
Institutions must reject:
- harmful revival paths,  
- dangerous reconstructions,  
- unsafe emergent behavior.

6.2 Human Oversight Logic
High‑impact reasoning requires:
- human review,  
- provenance validation,  
- explicit approval.

6.3 Emergent Behavior Boundaries
Emergent behavior is allowed only if:
- it remains safe,  
- it is logged,  
- it does not override constitutional principles.

Provenance is part of reasoning, not an afterthought.
