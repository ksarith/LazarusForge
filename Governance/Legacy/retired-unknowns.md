retired-unknowns.md

Legacy Module — Historical Unknowns, Lifecycle Completion, and Epistemic Closure in LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
The Retired Unknowns module preserves the full historical record of unknowns that have completed their lifecycle within LazarusForgeV0. Unknowns are first‑class epistemic objects — they shape reasoning, influence revival and reconstruction, and determine verification readiness.

When an unknown is retired, it becomes part of the Forge’s institutional memory, ensuring:

- epistemic continuity  
- provenance completeness  
- historical traceability  
- architectural ancestry  
- responsible uncertainty management  

Retired unknowns cannot influence current governance or operational logic. They exist solely for historical insight and provenance.

---

2. Scope
This module governs:

- unknowns that reached lifecycle completion  
- unknowns resolved through evidence  
- unknowns resolved through structural reconstruction  
- unknowns retired due to safety constraints  
- unknowns retired due to architectural changes  
- unknowns deprecated by canonical evolution  

This module does not govern:

- active unknowns  
- unknown lifecycle rules  
- unknown escalation  
- unknown dependency mapping  

Those are defined in Unknown Management.

---

3. Retirement Principles
Retired unknowns follow five principles:

- Lifecycle Completion — unknowns must complete their lifecycle before retirement.  
- Provenance Preservation — all unknowns must retain full historical records.  
- Non‑Interference — retired unknowns cannot affect active architecture.  
- Safety Alignment — unsafe unknowns must be clearly marked.  
- Contextualization — retirement must include justification and historical context.

These principles ensure safe and meaningful archival.

---

4. Unknown Retirement Categories
Unknowns may be retired for several reasons:

4.1 Resolved Unknowns
Unknowns resolved through:

- evidence maturation  
- dependency stabilization  
- structural reconstruction  
- verification gate passage  

4.2 Deprecated Unknowns
Unknowns made irrelevant due to:

- architectural changes  
- module restructuring  
- canonical evolution  
- removal of dependent elements  

4.3 Safety‑Retired Unknowns
Unknowns retired because they:

- violated safety boundaries  
- created unsafe revival paths  
- destabilized reconstruction  
- triggered oversight intervention  

4.4 Obsolete Unknowns
Unknowns that no longer matter due to:

- replacement by more mature logic  
- consolidation of related unknowns  
- removal of outdated modules  

Each category must include provenance and retirement justification.

---

5. Retirement Structure
Each retired unknown must follow a consistent archival structure:

`

Unknown Name (Retired)

Original Description
Explain what the unknown represented.

Category
Resolved / Deprecated / Safety‑Retired / Obsolete

Reason for Retirement
Explain why the unknown was retired.

Lifecycle Summary
Describe how the unknown progressed through its lifecycle.

Provenance Record
List evidence, reasoning trails, dependency maps, and oversight decisions.

Architectural Impact
Explain how the unknown influenced the Forge historically.

Replacement (if applicable)
Link to the element that resolved or superseded the unknown.

Retirement Date
YYYY‑MM‑DD
`

This structure ensures clarity and historical continuity.

---

6. Provenance Requirements
Retired unknowns must preserve:

- original declaration  
- lifecycle progression  
- dependency relationships  
- reasoning trails  
- evidence lineage  
- oversight decisions  
- retirement justification  

Provenance ensures epistemic accountability.

---

7. Safety Requirements
Unknowns retired for safety reasons must be clearly marked:

- Safety‑Retired  
- Unsafe Revival Path  
- Unsafe Reconstruction Path  
- Emergent Behavior Risk  
- Verification Failure  

Safety markings prevent accidental reuse.

---

8. Integration with Legacy Layer
Retired unknowns belong to the /governance/legacy directory:

`

        deprecated-modules.md

`

They interact with:

- archived-rules.md  
- deprecated-modules.md  

Together, these files form the Forge’s historical memory.
