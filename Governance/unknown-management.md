unknown-management.md

Constitutional Framework for Epistemic Uncertainty, Lifecycle Governance, and Architectural Dependency Tracking in LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
Unknown Management defines how LazarusForgeV0 recognizes, governs, tracks, and resolves epistemic uncertainty. Unknowns are treated as architectural dependencies, not informational gaps. Their lifecycle determines how the Forge matures, how revival processes proceed, and how institutional reasoning evaluates evidence.

Unknown Management is a constitutional layer, not an operational module.

---

2. Constitutional Role of Unknowns
Unknowns serve four institutional functions:

- Epistemic Integrity — preventing silent drift or untracked assumptions  
- Architectural Stability — ensuring revival and reconstruction do not rely on unverified premises  
- Governance Transparency — making uncertainty explicit, owned, and accountable  
- Maturity Tracking — enabling structured evolution from exploration to specification  

Unknowns are governance objects with obligations, dependencies, and lifecycle rules.

---

3. Unknown Definition
An unknown is any architectural element where:

- evidence is incomplete,  
- provenance is unclear,  
- dependencies are unresolved,  
- revival paths are ambiguous,  
- institutional reasoning cannot guarantee correctness,  
- verification gates cannot yet be passed.

Unknowns are not errors.  
They are signals that guide epistemic development.

---

4. Unknown Classes
Unknowns must be classified into one of the following constitutional categories:

4.1 Structural Unknowns
Missing or unclear architectural relationships, dependencies, or invariants.

4.2 Revival Unknowns
Ambiguities in reconstruction paths, fragment interpretation, or identity preservation.

4.3 Evidence Unknowns
Insufficient evidence to support a claim, specification, or architectural decision.

4.4 Provenance Unknowns
Unclear lineage, missing audit trails, or incomplete historical context.

4.5 Safety Unknowns
Uncertain risk boundaries, emergent behavior concerns, or unverified constraints.

4.6 Maturity Unknowns
Elements whose epistemic maturity cannot yet be determined.

Each unknown must belong to exactly one class.

---

5. Unknown Registration
Unknowns must be registered immediately when discovered.

Registration requires:

- unique identifier  
- class  
- description  
- evidence state  
- dependencies  
- owner  
- initial maturity level  
- date of registration  
- reason for declaration

Unknowns.md is the canonical registry.

---

6. Unknown Ownership
Every unknown must have a designated owner responsible for:

- tracking its evolution,  
- updating its maturity,  
- managing dependencies,  
- coordinating verification,  
- initiating retirement when resolved.

Ownership ensures accountability and prevents epistemic drift.

---

7. Unknown Lifecycle
Unknowns move through a constitutional lifecycle:

7.1 Declared
Unknown is registered and classified.

7.2 Investigated
Evidence gathering begins; dependencies are mapped.

7.3 Explored
Hypotheses, revival paths, or architectural interpretations are generated.

7.4 Verified
Unknown passes verification gates; evidence is sufficient.

7.5 Resolved
Unknown is fully understood, validated, and integrated.

7.6 Retired
Unknown is removed from the active registry but preserved in provenance history.

Unknowns may not skip lifecycle stages.

---

8. Dependency Mapping
Unknowns must explicitly list:

- upstream dependencies (what they rely on),  
- downstream dependencies (what relies on them),  
- cross‑module dependencies,  
- revival dependencies,  
- verification dependencies.

Dependency maps prevent accidental adoption of unresolved uncertainty.

---

9. Unknown Maturity
Unknowns must be assigned one of four maturity levels:

- Exploratory — speculative, unverified  
- Provisional — partially evidenced, not yet stable  
- Verified — passed verification gates  
- Canonical — fully resolved and integrated

Unknowns cannot become canonical without passing verification gates.

---

10. Verification Requirements
Unknowns must pass verification gates before resolution.

Verification includes:

- evidence evaluation  
- provenance validation  
- dependency resolution  
- safety assessment  
- institutional reasoning review  
- human oversight (for high‑impact unknowns)

Verification is constitutional, not optional.

---

11. Unknown Retirement
Unknowns may be retired only when:

- all dependencies are resolved,  
- evidence is complete,  
- provenance is documented,  
- institutional reasoning confirms stability,  
- verification gates are passed.

Retired unknowns remain in provenance history permanently.

---

12. Unknown Governance Obligations
Institutions must:

- maintain the unknown registry,  
- prevent silent unknown creation,  
- track unknown evolution,  
- ensure ownership continuity,  
- enforce lifecycle rules,  
- integrate unknowns into architectural maturity assessments.

Unknown governance is a constitutional obligation.

---

13. Integration with CIR
Unknown Management is directly governed by Computational Institutional Reasoning, which provides:

- epistemic logic,  
- evidence evaluation,  
- maturity classification,  
- reasoning cycle integration,  
- institutional memory requirements.

Unknowns are the operational manifestation of CIR’s uncertainty logic.

---

14. Integration with Safety
Unknowns must be flagged when they involve:

- emergent behavior,  
- risk boundaries,  
- revival hazards,  
- ambiguous identity reconstruction,  
- unclear agent interactions.

Safety unknowns require human oversight.

---

15. Integration with Provenance
Unknowns must maintain:

- full audit trails,  
- change history,  
- evidence lineage,  
- lifecycle transitions,  
- retirement records.

Provenance ensures institutional memory and prevents epistemic drift.

---

16. Constitutional Summary
Unknown Management ensures that LazarusForgeV0:

- treats uncertainty as a governance object,  
- maintains epistemic integrity,  
- evolves safely and coherently,  
- tracks architectural dependencies,  
- enforces verification gates,  
- preserves provenance,  
- integrates institutional reasoning,  
- prevents silent drift,  
- supports structured maturation.

Unknowns are the backbone of the Forge’s epistemic architecture.
