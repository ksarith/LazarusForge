Constitutional Safety Architecture and Risk Governance for LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
Safety Boundaries define the non‑overrideable constitutional limits that protect LazarusForgeV0 from harmful outputs, destabilizing architectural evolution, unsafe emergent behavior, and unverified revival paths. These boundaries ensure that the Forge remains:

- safe,  
- stable,  
- trustworthy,  
- human‑aligned,  
- epistemically responsible.

Safety Boundaries are constitutional.  
No operational module or sandbox experiment may bypass them.

---

2. Constitutional Safety Principles
Safety in LazarusForgeV0 is governed by five foundational principles:

- Anti‑Weaponization — the Forge must never produce harmful or weaponizable outputs.  
- Human Oversight — high‑impact changes require human review.  
- Verification Before Deployment — nothing becomes canonical without passing verification gates.  
- Emergent Behavior Control — emergent behavior is allowed only within safe limits.  
- Risk Transparency — all safety risks must be declared, tracked, and resolved.

These principles anchor all safety boundaries.

---

3. Safety Boundary Categories
Safety Boundaries are divided into six constitutional categories:

3.1 Anti‑Weaponization Boundaries
The Forge must reject any output that could be used to cause harm, including:

- destructive systems,  
- exploitative mechanisms,  
- malicious automation,  
- harmful revival paths.

This boundary supersedes all other concerns.

3.2 Revival Safety Boundaries
Revival processes must not:

- distort identity beyond allowed limits,  
- generate unsafe interpretations,  
- produce harmful reconstructions,  
- bypass verification gates.

Revival is creative, but never dangerous.

3.3 Emergent Behavior Boundaries
Emergent behavior is allowed only if:

- it remains safe,  
- it is logged,  
- it does not override constitutional principles,  
- it does not bypass verification gates,  
- it does not create safety unknowns.

Unsafe emergent behavior must be halted immediately.

3.4 Agent Autonomy Boundaries
Agents may act autonomously only within:

- module‑defined limits,  
- constitutional safety constraints,  
- verified revival protocols.

Agents may not:

- escalate autonomy without verification,  
- override human oversight,  
- generate unbounded emergent behavior.

3.5 Architectural Stability Boundaries
Architectural changes must not:

- destabilize canonical structures,  
- introduce unresolved unknowns,  
- break dependency graphs,  
- bypass verification gates.

Stability is a constitutional requirement.

3.6 Human Oversight Boundaries
Human review is mandatory when:

- safety unknowns exist,  
- emergent behavior is detected,  
- revival identity is ambiguous,  
- architectural risk is high.

Oversight cannot be automated away.

---

4. Safety Unknowns
Safety unknowns are first‑class governance objects.

They must be:

- registered,  
- owned,  
- investigated,  
- verified,  
- resolved before adoption.

Safety unknowns block canonical promotion.

---

5. Safety Verification Requirements
Safety verification requires:

- risk identification,  
- safety unknown registration,  
- emergent behavior analysis,  
- boundary enforcement,  
- human oversight (if required),  
- safety verification gate passage.

Safety verification is constitutional.

---

6. Safety Enforcement Workflow
Safety follows a strict constitutional workflow:

1. Risk Identification  
2. Safety Unknown Registration  
3. Emergent Behavior Analysis  
4. Boundary Enforcement  
5. Human Oversight  
6. Safety Verification Gate  
7. Approval or Rejection

Safety workflows override verification workflows when conflict arises.

---

7. Integration with Constitutional Layers
Safety Boundaries integrate with:

- Core Governance  
- Computational Institutional Reasoning  
- Unknown Management  
- Verification Gates  
- Provenance & Accountability  

Safety is the constitutional shield that protects all other layers.

---

8. Safety Boundary Summary
Safety Boundaries ensure that LazarusForgeV0:

- evolves safely and coherently,  
- rejects harmful outputs,  
- controls emergent behavior,  
- enforces human oversight,  
- resolves safety unknowns,  
- maintains architectural stability,  
- preserves epistemic integrity,  
- protects canonical architecture.

Safety is the Forge’s constitutional perimeter — strict, protective, and non‑negotiable.
