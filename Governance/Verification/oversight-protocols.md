Constitutional Human‑in‑the‑Loop Governance and High‑Impact Review Protocols for LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
Oversight Protocols define the constitutional requirements for human review, intervention, and approval within LazarusForgeV0. Oversight is mandatory for any high‑impact architectural change, safety‑critical decision, or epistemically sensitive evolution. These protocols ensure that the Forge remains:

- human‑aligned,  
- safe,  
- accountable,  
- epistemically grounded,  
- resistant to harmful emergent behavior.

Oversight is a constitutional safeguard.  
It cannot be bypassed by modules or sandbox logic.

---

2. Constitutional Oversight Principles
Oversight in LazarusForgeV0 is governed by five foundational principles:

- Human Primacy — humans retain final authority over high‑impact decisions.  
- Safety Priority — oversight is required whenever safety boundaries are involved.  
- Verification Dependency — oversight is integrated into verification gates.  
- Transparency and Accountability — oversight actions must be logged with provenance.  
- Intervention Authority — humans may halt, regress, or reject any element at any stage.

These principles anchor all oversight protocols.

---

3. Oversight Triggers
Oversight is required when any of the following conditions occur:

3.1 Safety Unknowns
Any unresolved safety unknown automatically triggers oversight.

3.2 High‑Impact Architectural Changes
Changes that affect:

- canonical structures,  
- module dependencies,  
- revival protocols,  
- reconstruction rules,  
- governance logic.

3.3 Emergent Behavior Detection
Oversight is mandatory when emergent behavior:

- exceeds safe boundaries,  
- influences architectural decisions,  
- creates new unknowns,  
- destabilizes reasoning.

3.4 Revival Identity Ambiguity
Oversight is required when:

- identity reconstruction is unclear,  
- revival paths diverge significantly,  
- evidence is insufficient for stable interpretation.

3.5 Canonical Promotion
Oversight is part of the final verification gate before canonical adoption.

3.6 Risk Escalation
Any escalation in:

- epistemic risk,  
- architectural instability,  
- dependency fragility.

Oversight must intervene.

---

4. Oversight Roles
Oversight is performed by designated human reviewers with constitutional authority.

4.1 Oversight Maintainers
Responsible for:

- reviewing high‑impact changes,  
- validating safety boundaries,  
- approving canonical promotion.

4.2 Governance Stewards
Responsible for:

- constitutional integrity,  
- provenance validation,  
- unknown lifecycle supervision.

4.3 Architectural Reviewers
Responsible for:

- dependency stability,  
- module coherence,  
- revival and reconstruction correctness.

These roles may overlap but cannot be automated.

---

5. Oversight Workflow
Oversight follows a structured constitutional workflow:

1. Trigger Detection  
2. Oversight Assignment  
3. Evidence Review  
4. Unknown Evaluation  
5. Safety Assessment  
6. Reasoning Trail Analysis  
7. Provenance Validation  
8. Decision Issued  
9. Provenance Logging

Oversight decisions must be documented permanently.

---

6. Oversight Decision Types
Oversight may issue one of four constitutional decisions:

6.1 Approval
Element may proceed to the next verification gate or canonical promotion.

6.2 Regression
Element must return to a lower maturity level due to:

- unresolved unknowns,  
- insufficient evidence,  
- safety concerns.

6.3 Revision Required
Element must be updated with:

- additional evidence,  
- improved reasoning trails,  
- resolved dependencies.

6.4 Rejection
Element is unsafe, unverifiable, or epistemically invalid.

Rejection must include justification and provenance.

---

7. Oversight and Verification Gates
Oversight is integrated into verification:

- Gate 4: Safety Boundary Gate — oversight required for safety unknowns.  
- Gate 5: Provenance Integrity Gate — oversight validates lineage.  
- Gate 6: Canonical Promotion Gate — oversight approves final adoption.

Oversight is part of verification, not separate from it.

---

8. Oversight and Unknown Management
Oversight must:

- validate unknown registration,  
- confirm lifecycle progression,  
- ensure dependency resolution,  
- approve unknown retirement.

Unknowns cannot advance maturity without oversight when safety or architectural risk is involved.

---

9. Oversight and Emergent Behavior
Oversight must intervene when emergent behavior:

- exceeds safe limits,  
- influences architectural decisions,  
- creates new unknowns,  
- destabilizes reasoning.

Oversight may halt emergent behavior immediately.

---

10. Oversight and Provenance
Oversight actions must be recorded in provenance:

- reviewer identity,  
- decision type,  
- justification,  
- evidence used,  
- unknowns evaluated,  
- safety assessments,  
- maturity changes.

Oversight without provenance is invalid.

---

11. Oversight Summary
Oversight Protocols ensure that LazarusForgeV0:

- remains human‑aligned,  
- evolves safely,  
- maintains epistemic integrity,  
- preserves provenance,  
- controls emergent behavior,  
- resolves unknowns responsibly,  
- protects canonical architecture.

Oversight is the Forge’s constitutional guardian — authoritative, transparent, and essential.
