Constitutional Epistemic Maturity Framework for Architectural Evolution in LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
Epistemic Maturity Classification defines how LazarusForgeV0 evaluates the developmental stage of any architectural element, including:

- revived fragments,  
- reconstructed structures,  
- module rules,  
- governance proposals,  
- unknowns,  
- evidence objects.

Maturity determines whether an element is exploratory, provisional, verified, or ready for canonical adoption.  
It is a constitutional requirement and cannot be bypassed.

---

2. Constitutional Role of Maturity
Maturity classification ensures that the Forge:

- evolves responsibly,  
- separates speculation from specification,  
- prevents premature adoption,  
- maintains epistemic integrity,  
- integrates verification gates,  
- preserves provenance,  
- resolves unknowns before promotion.

Maturity is the bridge between Computational Institutional Reasoning and Verification Gates.

---

3. Maturity Levels
Every architectural element must be assigned one of four constitutional maturity levels.

---

Level 1 — Exploratory
The element is speculative, creative, or experimental.

Characteristics:
- high uncertainty  
- unresolved unknowns  
- incomplete evidence  
- early reasoning trails  
- sandbox‑compatible  
- not eligible for verification

Requirements:
- unknown registration  
- evidence collection  
- reasoning trail initiation

Boundaries:
- cannot influence canonical architecture  
- cannot bypass safety constraints  
- cannot be used as a dependency for verified elements

---

Level 2 — Provisional
The element has partial evidence and structured reasoning but remains unstable.

Characteristics:
- some evidence available  
- unknowns partially resolved  
- reasoning trails maturing  
- dependencies mapped  
- verification preparation underway

Requirements:
- dependency mapping  
- evidence lineage documentation  
- CIR cycle completion (Observation → Interpretation → Exploration)

Boundaries:
- cannot be adopted  
- cannot override module rules  
- must pass verification gates to advance

---

Level 3 — Verified
The element has passed verification gates and is epistemically stable.

Characteristics:
- evidence sufficient  
- unknowns resolved or retired  
- reasoning trails complete  
- provenance validated  
- safety boundaries confirmed

Requirements:
- pass all verification gates  
- complete provenance record  
- safety unknown resolution  
- human oversight (if required)

Boundaries:
- eligible for canonical promotion  
- may serve as a dependency for other verified elements  
- cannot be modified without re‑verification

---

Level 4 — Canonical
The element is fully adopted into the Forge’s architecture.

Characteristics:
- verified and stable  
- provenance complete  
- architectural lineage established  
- no unresolved unknowns  
- safe and mature

Requirements:
- pass canonical promotion gate  
- institutional approval  
- permanent provenance preservation

Boundaries:
- cannot be altered without constitutional migration  
- cannot be overridden by modules or sandbox rules  
- forms part of the Forge’s long‑term identity

---

4. Maturity Advancement Rules
Elements must advance through maturity levels sequentially:

1. Exploratory  
2. Provisional  
3. Verified  
4. Canonical  

No element may skip levels.

Advancement requires:

- evidence maturation  
- unknown lifecycle progression  
- reasoning trail completion  
- verification gate passage  
- provenance validation  
- safety confirmation

---

5. Maturity Regression Rules
Elements may regress if:

- new unknowns emerge,  
- evidence becomes invalid,  
- provenance is incomplete,  
- safety boundaries are violated,  
- emergent behavior destabilizes the element.

Regression must be logged in provenance.

---

6. Maturity and Unknown Management
Unknowns directly influence maturity:

- unresolved unknowns → Exploratory or Provisional  
- resolved unknowns → Verified  
- retired unknowns → Canonical eligibility  

Unknown lifecycle progression is required for maturity advancement.

---

7. Maturity and Verification Gates
Verification gates determine whether an element can advance from:

- Provisional → Verified  
- Verified → Canonical  

Gate failures prevent advancement and must be recorded in provenance.

---

8. Maturity and Safety
Safety boundaries restrict maturity:

- safety unknowns block verification  
- emergent behavior may regress maturity  
- high‑risk elements require human oversight  
- unsafe revival paths cannot advance

Safety supersedes maturity.

---

9. Maturity and Provenance
Provenance must record:

- maturity level changes  
- reasoning trail evolution  
- evidence lineage updates  
- unknown lifecycle transitions  
- verification outcomes  
- regression events

Maturity without provenance is invalid.

---

10. Maturity Classification Summary
Epistemic Maturity ensures that LazarusForgeV0:

- evolves coherently,  
- separates exploration from specification,  
- maintains epistemic integrity,  
- resolves uncertainty responsibly,  
- enforces verification gates,  
- preserves provenance,  
- protects canonical architecture.

Maturity is the Forge’s developmental logic — structured, constitutional, and essential.
