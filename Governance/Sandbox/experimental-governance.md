Sandbox Governance — Safe Exploration, Controlled Experimentation, and Non‑Canonical Innovation in LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
The Experimental Governance module defines how LazarusForgeV0 conducts exploration, experimentation, prototyping, and speculative reasoning without destabilizing the canonical architecture. The sandbox is where new ideas can be tested — but never allowed to override constitutional governance.

Sandbox governance ensures that experimentation remains:

- safe  
- isolated  
- traceable  
- epistemically responsible  
- non‑canonical  
- reversible  

The sandbox is the Forge’s laboratory, not its legislature.

---

2. Scope
This module governs:

- experimental revival  
- prototype reconstruction  
- speculative modules  
- exploratory reasoning  
- experimental unknowns  
- temporary structures  
- draft governance ideas  
- non‑canonical workflows  

This module does not govern:

- constitutional principles  
- verification gates  
- canonical modules  
- provenance requirements for canonical files  

Those are defined in higher layers.

---

3. Sandbox Principles
Sandbox activity must follow five principles:

- Isolation — sandbox outputs cannot affect canonical architecture.  
- Safety — experiments must remain within safety boundaries.  
- Transparency — all uncertainty must be declared.  
- Traceability — all experiments must be logged.  
- Reversibility — sandbox changes must be easy to revert.

These principles ensure safe exploration.

---

4. Sandbox Structure
The sandbox lives under:

`
/governance
    /sandbox
        experimental-governance.md
        drafts/
            *.md
`

4.1 experimental-governance.md
Defines rules for sandbox behavior.

4.2 drafts/
Contains:

- experimental modules  
- revival prototypes  
- reconstruction prototypes  
- speculative governance ideas  
- temporary structures  
- exploratory reasoning trails  

Drafts are never canonical.

---

5. Sandbox Activity Types
Sandbox activity falls into four categories:

5.1 Experimental Revival
Testing new revival heuristics or identity interpretations.

5.2 Prototype Reconstruction
Trying alternative reconstruction patterns or structural logic.

5.3 Speculative Modules
Designing hypothetical modules that may later become operational.

5.4 Governance Experiments
Exploring new governance concepts, verification ideas, or safety models.

All experiments must be logged.

---

6. Sandbox Boundaries
Sandbox activity must remain within strict boundaries:

6.1 No Canonical Influence
Sandbox outputs cannot:

- modify canonical files  
- override constitutional governance  
- bypass verification gates  
- alter operational modules  

6.2 No Unsafe Behavior
Sandbox experiments must not:

- violate safety boundaries  
- generate harmful outputs  
- create unbounded emergent behavior  
- destabilize architecture  

6.3 No Silent Unknowns
All sandbox unknowns must be:

- declared  
- registered  
- owned  
- tracked  

Unknowns cannot be ignored.

6.4 No Permanent Structures
Sandbox outputs must remain:

- temporary  
- reversible  
- non‑canonical  

Sandbox is for exploration, not permanence.

---

7. Sandbox Workflow
Sandbox activity follows a structured workflow:

Step 1 — Proposal
Contributor proposes an experiment.

Step 2 — Unknown Declaration
Contributor declares all uncertainty.

Step 3 — Draft Creation
Contributor creates a draft in /sandbox/drafts.

Step 4 — Experimentation
Contributor tests revival, reconstruction, or governance ideas.

Step 5 — Logging
Contributor logs:

- reasoning trails  
- evidence  
- unknowns  
- outcomes  

Step 6 — Evaluation
Contributor evaluates whether the experiment:

- should be retired  
- should be refined  
- should be proposed for verification  
- should remain in sandbox  

Step 7 — Optional Promotion
If promising, the experiment may be proposed for:

- verification  
- reconstruction  
- operational module integration  

Promotion requires verification gates and oversight.

---

8. Unknown Management Integration
Sandbox unknowns follow Unknown Management rules:

- declare unknowns  
- assign ownership  
- map dependencies  
- track lifecycle  
- escalate unresolved unknowns  
- retire unknowns with provenance  

Unknowns are first‑class sandbox objects.

---

9. Safety Integration
Sandbox experiments must follow Safety Boundaries:

- detect unsafe behavior  
- classify safety unknowns  
- halt dangerous experiments  
- escalate risk  
- avoid harmful revival or reconstruction paths  

Safety supersedes experimentation.

---

10. Provenance Integration
Sandbox provenance must include:

- experiment logs  
- reasoning trails  
- evidence lineage  
- unknown lifecycle updates  
- outcomes  
- retirement or promotion decisions  

Sandbox provenance is separate from canonical provenance.

---

11. Promotion Path
Sandbox experiments may be promoted only if:

- they pass verification gates  
- they resolve unknowns  
- they meet safety boundaries  
- they maintain architectural stability  
- they receive oversight approval  

Promotion path:

`
Sandbox → Provisional Module → Verified Module → Canonical Module
`

No experiment may skip steps.

---

12. Retirement Path
Sandbox experiments may be retired if:

- they fail verification  
- they violate safety boundaries  
- they introduce unresolved unknowns  
- they destabilize architecture  
- they become obsolete  

Retired experiments must be logged in:

- archived-rules.md  
- deprecated-modules.md  
- retired-unknowns.md
