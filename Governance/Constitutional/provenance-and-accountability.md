Constitutional Framework for Audit Integrity, Architectural Lineage, and Institutional Responsibility in LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
Provenance and Accountability define the constitutional guarantees that ensure LazarusForgeV0 maintains:

- trustworthy audit trails,  
- transparent architectural lineage,  
- responsible governance behavior,  
- traceable decision history,  
- institutional memory that prevents epistemic drift.

This document establishes how the Forge remembers, records, and justifies its evolution.

---

2. Constitutional Mission
This charter ensures that LazarusForgeV0:

- preserves the full history of its reasoning,  
- maintains accountability for all changes,  
- documents evidence lineage,  
- tracks unknown lifecycles,  
- enforces responsible governance actions,  
- protects canonical stability through traceability.

Provenance is a constitutional requirement, not an operational feature.

---

3. Provenance Principles
Provenance governs how the Forge records and preserves its epistemic and architectural history.

3.1 Total Traceability
Every architectural element must have:

- a clear origin,  
- a documented evolution,  
- a verified lineage,  
- a complete audit trail.

Nothing may enter the canonical architecture without provenance.

3.2 Immutable History
Provenance records cannot be altered retroactively.

Corrections must be appended, not overwritten.

3.3 Evidence Lineage
All evidence must include:

- source,  
- class,  
- maturity,  
- verification status,  
- dependency relationships.

Evidence lineage ensures epistemic integrity.

3.4 Reasoning Trails
Institutional reasoning must be recorded, including:

- hypotheses,  
- interpretations,  
- verification outcomes,  
- rejected alternatives,  
- uncertainty declarations.

Reasoning trails prevent silent drift.

3.5 Architectural Ancestry
Every canonical element must document:

- its ancestry,  
- its dependencies,  
- its revival path (if applicable),  
- its verification history.

Ancestry ensures long‑term coherence.

---

4. Accountability Principles
Accountability governs how institutions and contributors behave.

4.1 Responsibility for Change
Every change must have:

- an accountable owner,  
- a justification,  
- a provenance entry,  
- a verification record.

No anonymous or unowned changes are permitted.

4.2 Governance Responsibility
Institutions must:

- maintain provenance,  
- enforce verification gates,  
- track unknowns,  
- preserve architectural lineage,  
- uphold safety constraints.

Governance responsibility is constitutional.

4.3 Contributor Accountability
Contributors must:

- document reasoning,  
- justify changes,  
- maintain provenance,  
- register unknowns,  
- follow verification workflows.

Accountability is required for participation.

4.4 Institutional Memory
Institutions must preserve:

- decisions,  
- evidence,  
- unknown lifecycles,  
- architectural migrations,  
- safety assessments.

Memory prevents epistemic collapse.

---

5. Provenance Objects
Provenance is composed of constitutional objects:

- Evidence Records  
- Unknown Lifecycle Entries  
- Verification Gate Logs  
- Safety Assessments  
- Architectural Lineage Maps  
- Reasoning Trails  
- Change Histories

Each object must be preserved permanently.

---

6. Provenance Lifecycle
Provenance follows a constitutional lifecycle:

6.1 Creation
Provenance is created when:

- evidence is introduced,  
- unknowns are declared,  
- reasoning begins,  
- revival paths are explored,  
- changes are proposed.

6.2 Expansion
Provenance expands as:

- evidence matures,  
- unknowns evolve,  
- verification gates are passed,  
- safety assessments occur.

6.3 Institutionalization
Provenance becomes canonical when:

- verification is complete,  
- safety is confirmed,  
- architectural lineage is stable.

6.4 Preservation
Provenance is preserved indefinitely.

Nothing may erase it.

---

7. Accountability Lifecycle
Accountability follows a parallel lifecycle:

7.1 Assignment
Every change, unknown, or architectural element must have an owner.

7.2 Responsibility
Owners must maintain:

- evidence,  
- reasoning,  
- provenance,  
- verification readiness.

7.3 Review
High‑impact changes require human oversight.

7.4 Resolution
Owners must resolve unknowns, complete verification, and finalize provenance.

7.5 Retirement
Retired elements remain in provenance history.
