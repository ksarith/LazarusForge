Constitutional Framework for Modular Architecture, Controlled Dependencies, and Canonical Stability in LazarusForgeV0

---

1. Purpose
The Operational Modules Framework defines how modules are created, structured, governed, and integrated within LazarusForgeV0. Modules provide operational functionality, but they must remain subordinate to:

- Constitutional Governance  
- Verification & Safety Constraints  
- Computational Institutional Reasoning  
- Unknown Management  
- Provenance & Accountability

Modules extend the Forge — they do not define it.

---

2. Constitutional Position of Modules
Operational modules occupy the third tier of the governance hierarchy:

1. Constitutional Governance  
2. Verification & Safety Constraints  
3. Operational Modules  
4. Experimental Sandbox  
5. Archived Legacy

Modules may reference constitutional layers but may not override them.

---

3. Module Mission
Modules exist to:

- provide operational logic,  
- implement revival and reconstruction mechanisms,  
- support architectural workflows,  
- encode domain‑specific behavior,  
- extend the Forge without destabilizing it.

Modules are operational, not constitutional.

---

4. Module Principles
Modules must follow these principles:

4.1 Subordination to Constitutional Layers
Modules may not contradict or override:

- constitutional principles,  
- verification gates,  
- safety constraints,  
- unknown lifecycle rules,  
- provenance requirements.

4.2 Controlled Dependencies
Modules may reference:

- constitutional governance,  
- verification & safety constraints,  
- CIR,  
- unknown management,  
- provenance.

Modules may reference each other only through acyclic dependency graphs.

4.3 Isolation of Experimental Logic
Experimental behavior must be placed in the sandbox, not in modules.

4.4 Evidence‑First Design
Modules must justify their rules with evidence and reasoning trails.

4.5 Provenance Preservation
All module changes must maintain provenance and accountability.

---

5. Module Structure Template
Every module must follow a standard structure:

5.1 Title
Clear, descriptive module name.

5.2 Purpose
What the module does and why it exists.

5.3 Scope
What the module governs and what it does not govern.

5.4 Rules
Operational rules implemented by the module.

5.5 Dependencies
Required references to:

- constitutional governance,  
- verification constraints,  
- CIR,  
- unknown management,  
- provenance,  
- other modules (acyclic only).

5.6 Exceptions
Conditions where module rules do not apply.

5.7 Unknowns
Unknowns created, managed, or resolved by the module.

5.8 Verification Requirements
Verification gates required before module rules become canonical.

5.9 Provenance
Audit trail and lineage for module creation and evolution.

5.10 Version History
Semantic versioning and change log.

This template ensures consistency across all modules.

---

6. Module Categories
Modules fall into constitutional‑aligned categories:

6.1 Revival Modules
Define how fragments are revived, interpreted, or reconstructed.

6.2 Reconstruction Modules
Define how revived content is structured, stabilized, or matured.

6.3 Workflow Modules
Define contributor workflows, routing, discovery, and architectural processes.

6.4 Integrity Modules
Define repository integrity, consistency checks, and architectural invariants.

6.5 Interaction Modules
Define agent behavior, communication protocols, and cooperative logic.

6.6 Governance Modules
Define operational governance processes (not constitutional ones).

Each category must follow the module template.

---

7. Module Dependency Rules
Modules must follow strict dependency rules:

7.1 Acyclic Dependency Graph
Modules may depend on other modules only if the dependency graph remains acyclic.

7.2 Constitutional Anchoring
All modules must anchor to:

- constitutional governance,  
- verification constraints,  
- CIR,  
- unknown management,  
- provenance.

7.3 No Circular Governance
Modules may not reference each other in circular governance patterns.

7.4 Dependency Transparency
All dependencies must be declared explicitly.

---

8. Module Verification
Modules must pass verification gates before becoming canonical.

Verification includes:

- evidence evaluation,  
- dependency stability checks,  
- unknown resolution,  
- safety assessment,  
- provenance validation,  
- human oversight (if required).

Modules cannot bypass verification.

---

9. Module Unknown Management
Modules must integrate unknown management:

- declare unknowns,  
- track unknown lifecycle,  
- resolve unknown dependencies,  
- retire unknowns with provenance.

Unknowns created by modules must follow constitutional lifecycle rules.

---

10. Module Provenance
Modules must maintain:

- full audit trails,  
- reasoning trails,  
- evidence lineage,  
- version history,  
- architectural ancestry.

Provenance ensures accountability and long‑term stability.

---

11. Module Lifecycle
Modules follow a constitutional lifecycle:

11.1 Draft
Created in the sandbox.

11.2 Proposed
Submitted for verification.

11.3 Verified
Passed verification gates.

11.4 Canonical
Integrated into the operational module layer.

11.5 Deprecated
Marked for retirement.

11.6 Archived
Moved to legacy with preserved provenance.

Modules may not skip lifecycle stages.

---

12. Integration with Constitutional Layers
Modules must integrate with:

- Core Governance  
- Computational Institutional Reasoning  
- Unknown Management Constitution  
- Verification & Safety Constraints  
- Provenance & Accountability Charter

Modules extend the Forge but do not define its epistemic identity.

---

13. Operational Summary
The Operational Modules Framework ensures that LazarusForgeV0:

- evolves modularly and coherently,  
- maintains constitutional integrity,  
- preserves provenance,  
- enforces verification,  
- manages unknowns responsibly,  
- supports controlled architectural growth,  
- prevents destabilizing dependencies,  
- isolates experimental behavior,  
- maintains long‑term stability.

Modules are the operational machinery of the Forge — governed, verified, and accountable.
