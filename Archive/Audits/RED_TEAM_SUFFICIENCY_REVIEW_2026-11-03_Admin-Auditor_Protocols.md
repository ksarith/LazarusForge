## The current landscape (as of 2026-11-03)

- **NIST AI 600-1 — Generative AI Profile** (published 2024-07-26): companion to the AI RMF 1.0; names 12 GenAI-specific risk categories (confabulation, prompt injection, data poisoning, supply-chain, …) with 211 suggested actions across Govern/Map/Measure/Manage; explicitly recommends adversarial testing / red teaming as a Measure. Sources: [Anaconda — NIST AI 600-1 explained](https://www.anaconda.com/blog/nist-ai-600-1-generative-ai-profile-guide), [AI Alert — the 12 risk categories](https://ai-alert.org/posts/nist-generative-ai-profile-600-1-explained/).
- **NIST AI 100-2 E2025** (March 2025): the adversarial-machine-learning attack taxonomy — the US federal baseline for adversarial testing structure. Source: [Mindgard — What is AI red teaming](https://mindgard.ai/blog/what-is-ai-red-teaming).
- **MITRE ATLAS** (v4.5.0): the ATT&CK-style knowledge base of adversary tactics/techniques against AI systems; in 2026 usage it is mapped to agentic and MCP-specific attacks for red-team operations. Sources: [Repello — ATLAS mapped to red-team ops](https://repello.ai/blog/mitre-atlas-framework), [TheCyberThrone — guide to AI red teaming with ATLAS](https://thecyberthrone.in/2026/03/27/guide-to-ai-red-teaming-with-mitre-atlas/).
- **OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026** (published 2025-12-09 by the OWASP GenAI Security Project; 100+ security experts; ASI01–ASI10): the first formal industry ranking of agent-specific security risks, complementing the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications and the OWASP GenAI Red Teaming Guide. Source: [Cycode — OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications 2026](https://cycode.com/blog/owasp-top-10-agentic-applications/).
- **ISO/IEC 42001**: the first certifiable AI management system (AIMS) standard; insurers and auditors now expect documented AI red-teaming evidence mappable to it. Sources: [DSalta — red teaming for SOC 2 / ISO 42001 / NIST](https://www.dsalta.com/resources/ai-compliance/ai-red-teaming-compliance-soc-2-iso-42001-nist), [Mend — ISO 42001 explained](https://www.mend.io/blog/iso-42001/).
- **EU AI Act**: Article 55 obliges providers of GPAI models with systemic risk to perform model evaluations **including adversarial testing**; Article 9 risk-management obligations bring adversarial testing to high-risk AI systems (with the August 2026 compliance horizon), under penalties up to EUR 35M / 7% of turnover. Sources: [Praxikon — Article 55](https://www.praxikon.com/en/verplichtingen/article-55-gpai-systemic-risk), [Cybersecurity Switzerland — EU AI Act red teaming](https://cybersecurityswitzerland.com/encyclopedia/eu-ai-act-security/).

## Sufficiency verdict

**Sufficient as-is for its actual risk surface — with three named gaps the human should rule on.**

AP's risk surface is not a deployed AI system; it is the **governance integrity of an AI-assisted doctrine corpus** — prompt-injection, model exfiltration and jailbreak taxonomies (the center of NIST AI 600-1, ATLAS, OWASP and the EU AI Act) are mostly orthogonal to what AP must defend, which is: fabricated provenance, stale attestation, self-validation, incentive drift and correlated error among contributing agents. On that surface, AP is **at or above peer industry practice**: the AP-017 cold-session closure bar is stricter than typical industry separation; trace preservation matches; EIP is a disclosure-program analog; cadence and degradation doctrine are continuous rather than point-in-time — the latter alone exceeds much industry practice, which still treats red teaming as a pre-launch event.

The three gaps, in priority order:

1. **No coverage ledger (dimension 6).** "Classes applied: all ten" is a claim, not a measurement. Highest-value, lowest-cost fix: a coverage ledger (class × target × date × pass/fail/inconclusive — the R13 result vocabulary already exists) so Battery coverage becomes quantitative and trendable. The Drift Indicator "Battery applications produce zero findings across two consecutive cycles" begs for exactly this data.
2. **No taxonomy crosswalk (dimensions 1 and 9).** The Battery is failure-mode-driven and the industry standard is adversary-technique-driven; without a one-time mapping of AP's ten classes to ASI01–10 and the ATLAS tactics, coverage claims cannot be stated in industry vocabulary, and techniques outside both axes (e.g., supply-chain on the corpus tooling itself) stay invisible. A one-time crosswalk document, human-ratified, closes this without touching doctrine.
3. **Independence defined across three vocabularies, with the strongest bar currently unsatisfied in practice (dimension 3; Battery Class 3/4 of CAMPAIGN3_AUDIT_04).** Industry treats red-team independence as binary and structural; AP splits it across Rule 8 ("a different agent" — undefined), AP-017 (no session context) and GOV-008 (diversity preference — NOT satisfied, disclosed). Until Rule 8's term is tightened or bound to the other two, a same-architecture actor can be form-compliant — this session is the live demonstration.

None of the three blocks current operation; all three are additive (no doctrine rewrite). External attestation (dimension 8) is the only dimension where AP is below the standard *by design* rather than by gap — the sovereignty model is a legitimate choice the human has already made, and it should simply be recorded as such when the corpus's trust posture is next reviewed.
